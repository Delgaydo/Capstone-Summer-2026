### Chapter 1: Introduction

#### 1.1 Background of the Study

 Mathematics achievement in U.S. high schools continues to be an important topic in education policy and school improvement efforts. In Florida, the Algebra 1 End-of-Course (EOC) exam plays a major role because it is required for high school graduation and is often used as a measure of student readiness for higher-level mathematics courses. According to Holley (2017), the Algebra 1 EOC functions as a “comparative test model,” where student success depends on how well they perform compared to other test takers, with passing rates reported as low as 19%. Because of the importance placed on this exam, EOC scores can affect student placement, motivation, and district accountability. As a result, it is important to better understand the factors that influence student performance across different groups of students. 
Traditional regression models mainly focus on average student performance, which can hide important differences between low-performing, average-performing, and high-performing students. However, previous research has shown that many predictors of achievement do not affect all students equally. Han and Whitacre (2016) found that school size had a stronger negative effect on higher-performing students in Oklahoma high schools, while Pani and Kishore (2016) found that absenteeism had a greater negative impact on lower-performing students. Other studies have also shown that demographic and socioeconomic factors, such as ethnicity and family background, can affect achievement differently across performance levels. 
Although there has been growing research using quantile-based methods in education, few studies have applied quantile regression to Florida’s Algebra 1 EOC outcomes. Holley (2017) examined Florida EOC performance using traditional statistical methods, but did not use quantile regression. This creates an important gap in the literature because Florida’s diverse student population, differences in socioeconomic conditions, and variation in school characteristics may influence students differently depending on their achievement level. 
This study will address that gap by using quantile regression to examine how demographic, socioeconomic, and school-level factors predict Algebra 1 EOC achievement across different levels of student performance in Florida high schools. By going beyond average effects, the study aims to determine whether factors such as poverty, minority enrollment, absenteeism, and school size affect struggling students differently than higher-achieving students. Understanding these differences can help educators and policymakers develop targeted interventions, improve educational equity, and better support student success in mathematics across Florida.
